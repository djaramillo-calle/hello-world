# FRICTION-MINIMAL INPUT ENGINE — Daily English Routine

**For:** an intermediate adult learner, conversation-focused, 45–60 min/weekday, anchors = morning / lunch / before bed.
**Phase placement:** functional intermediate ≈ Phase 2 of the review's protocol ("Volume, narrowly aimed," 50–300 h) — its 65/25/10 input/interaction/study split is the reference this plan deliberately bends.

## 1. Philosophy (3 sentences), argued honestly

Adherence is the binding constraint: the review itself concludes that "the variance between committed learners is dominated by hours and interaction quality, not by which of two reasonable methods they picked," so the winning plan is the one whose median day costs almost no willpower. Therefore this plan runs a large narrow-input engine on autopilot (decisions made once per topic rotation, never daily), a tiny daily retrieval habit, and holds interaction at the evidence-based floor rather than maximizing it. **Strongest evidence for:** massive comprehensible input reliably builds comprehension, vocabulary, and implicit knowledge (●●● in the review), implicit exposure wins on delayed tests (g = 1.76 vs 0.77 explicit), and spacing/retrieval beat massed hours — all of which reward exactly what low-friction routines produce: sustained, distributed volume. **Strongest evidence against — stated plainly:** interaction beats reception at equal time-on-task for speaking; French immersion students reached near-native comprehension and stayed conspicuously weak in production (the Swain finding), and the 2025 neuro-ecological work found interactive sessions beat equal hours of non-interactive listening on *both* speaking and comprehension. This plan accepts slower speaking gains as the price of a routine that actually survives, and hedges by keeping interaction at a hard floor (§4) instead of zero — the pure-input doctrine's speaking prohibition is the part of the CI movement the evidence contradicts most directly.

## 2. Weekly template (Mon–Fri + light weekend)

| | Morning (≈10 min) | Lunch (≈15–25 min) | Evening / before bed (≈15–35 min) | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Mon** | SRS reviews, answers spoken **aloud** — 10 | Narrow listening, current topic playlist — 25 | Narrow reading, same topic — 15; harvest 3–5 items into SRS + learn them aloud — 5 | 55 |
| **Tue** | SRS aloud — 10 | Narrow listening — 25 | **AI voice conversation** (prompt-configured, §4) — 20 | 55 |
| **Wed** | SRS aloud — 10 | Narrow listening — 25 | **Read-aloud/shadow sandwich** on a transcript paragraph from this week's listening — 15; harvest — 5 | 55 |
| **Thu** | SRS aloud — 10 | Narrow listening — 15 | Read-aloud warm-up, 3 passes of one paragraph — 5; **tutor/exchange session** (standing booking) — 30 | 60 |
| **Fri** | SRS aloud — 10 | Narrow listening — 25 | Week close: harvest, 2-line log, refill/rotate playlist queue — 15 | 50 |
| **Sat/Sun (optional)** | — | — | Extensive viewing in-topic 30–60 min; monthly authentic-audio check (10 min); diagnostic on cycle weekends | 0–70 |

Weekly weekday load ≈ 275 min: input ≈ 130 (47%), retrieval/study ≈ 65 (24%), sound/articulation work ≈ 35 (13%), interaction ≈ 50 (18%, plus uncounted free work-English). Honest note: 18% interaction is below the Phase-2 recommendation of 25% — that is the philosophy's tradeoff, partially offset by English at work.

Evidence tags: SRS-aloud [spacing/retrieval; production-format beats recognition; production effect]; lunch engine [narrow listening; comprehensible input volume]; evening placement of new learning [sleep consolidation]; sandwich [shadowing + read-aloud, used exactly in the "lane" the review defines]; Thu warm-up [Phase 3: "warm up the mouth before sessions"]; two conversations [Phase 2: "two conversations a week minimum"].

## 3. Input architecture

**Topic choice.** Pick 4 topics the learner will genuinely converse about — the review's rule: "your work, your city, your family, your opinions." Concretely: (a) their professional field, (b) one hobby/passion, (c) their city/country and current events there, (d) everyday-life/relationships content. [Narrow listening: "maps perfectly onto the real goal."]

**Autopilot rotation.** One decision per rotation, zero per day: subscribe to 2–3 podcasts/YouTube creators per topic, auto-download into one ordered playlist. Two topics active at a time — primary feeds lunch listening, secondary feeds Monday reading. Rotate a topic out when a *fresh* episode is ≥90% comprehensible without transcript, or after ~15–20 hours, whichever first; rotation happens during the Friday week-close slot only. Narrow-listening transfer to untrained topics is documented, so rotating is safe.

**Sleep-consolidation slot.** All *new* material is learned in the before-bed slot: the day's harvested words/chunks get their first spoken retrieval there, and the shadow sandwich (sound-category work) sits there too. Morning SRS then retests them after a night's sleep — a built-in overnight spacing interval.

**SRS fed from input, not word lists.** During or after listening, star 3–5 words or whole chunks actually met that day (chunks preferred: *it depends on…, that's a fair point, I was going to say*). Card format is **production-aloud**: front = meaning cue plus the source sentence with the item blanked; the learner *says* the answer before flipping. No recognition cards. Cap: ~25 new items/week so morning reviews stay ≤10 min. If nothing was harvested, the deck runs on backlog; the engine never blocks on harvesting.

**Read-aloud/shadow sandwich (2×/week, Wed + Fri-optional; warm-up variant Thu).** One paragraph from the transcript of an episode already listened to: read aloud cold → shadow the audio a beat behind → read aloud again. The middle step supplies the pronunciation model plain read-aloud lacks; the material recycles the week's narrow vocabulary.

**Conditional branch:** if the diagnostic's dictation module reports a low speed ceiling (decode fails below ~1.2×), swap one weekly sandwich for 15 min of minimal-pair perception work on the failing contrasts until the ceiling moves. [HVPT: "if you cannot reliably hear a distinction, every hour of listening input is partially wasted."]

## 4. Minimum interaction quota

The review's floor at this phase is **two conversations a week**; this plan keeps exactly two, engineered for the lowest possible friction:

1. **Tuesday evening, 20 min AI voice conversation** — no scheduling, no social cost, and its best-evidenced effect (anxiety reduction, willingness to communicate) is precisely the on-ramp function. Standing system prompt, per the review's instruction: *"When I make an error, don't correct me. Signal that you didn't understand and make me try again. Every ten turns, list the three errors I repeated."* Topic = whatever this week's playlist covered, so input vocabulary gets its first productive outing.
2. **Thursday evening, 30 min human session** — a *standing, prepaid, recurring* tutor or exchange booking (same person, same time, forever; booking friction paid once). Tutor briefed with the same script: flag breakdowns, force self-repair, no smooth reformulation.

English at work (meetings, colleagues) and English speakers in the personal circle are treated as free bonus interaction above the quota — encouraged, never scheduled, never counted, so a quiet work week can't break the plan.

## 5. Minimum-viable day (≤15 min)

Two actions, both attached to existing anchors: **(a)** morning SRS reviews aloud, reviews only, no new cards (~8 min); **(b)** press play on the downloaded playlist and attend for ~7 min (lunch or bed — either anchor counts). Definition of done is literally "reviews cleared + play pressed." No harvest, no sandwich; a missed Tuesday AI session is simply skipped; a missed tutor session converts per §8. Background/unattended audio does not count. This preserves the two mechanisms with the strongest distribution evidence (spaced retrieval + daily input contact) at a cost low enough to survive illness, travel, and terrible days.

## 6. Measurement hooks

- **Week 1:** run `diagnostic.html` once (~60 min, done on the first weekend or split across two evenings), press "Copy result row," append to `tracking.tsv`. This is the baseline; nothing in the plan is tuned until it exists.
- **Cycle:** re-run every 5 weeks, same conditions, log a new row. Respect the noise thresholds — movement below ±8 C-test pts, ±1,100 families, ±10 dictation pts, ±12 wpm, ±8 MTLD is not progress. Repeat scores are floors (item memory).
- **Monthly authentic-audio check:** use a never-heard episode from a *rotated-out or untrained* topic (this doubles as the narrow-listening transfer test); log the real-speech penalty vs the synthetic dictation score.
- **Quarterly:** free EF SET as the external anchor.
- **Predicted metric movement:** the input engine + harvest SRS should move `vocab_size` and `dict_pct` (and the dictation speed ceiling) first, `ctest_pct` next; the shadow work supports `dict_pct` and `ei_pct`; the interaction quota plus work English should move `evidence_n` and, slowly, `beyond_core_pct`.
- **The metric this plan will predictably move LEAST: `wpm` (speech rate), and with it filler ratio.** Speech rate is built by production repetition under time pressure (4/3/2), which this plan excludes to protect friction budget. Expect wpm to sit inside its ±12 noise band for the first one or two cycles. This is the immersion-production-gap cost made local and visible. Escalation rule: if wpm shows no signal after **two** consecutive cycles while vocab/dictation move, add one weekly 12-min 4/3/2 self-recording (Saturday, phone voice memo) — the cheapest fluency-specific intervention in the review.

## 7. Onboarding, weeks 1–2

**Week 1 — stand up the engine, measure nothing by feel.**
- Day 1 (evening, ~30 min one-time setup): choose the 4 topics; subscribe 2–3 shows per active topic; build the auto-download playlist; save the AI-partner system prompt.
- Day 2 (evening, ~20 min): create the SRS deck with the production-aloud card template; seed it with 10 conversation-management chunks.
- Days 2–5: run the lunch-listening and morning-SRS anchors only (partial template, ~35 min/day) to set the habit before adding anything else.
- Day 3: book the standing Thursday tutor slot (recurring, prepaid if possible).
- Weekend: run `diagnostic.html` (~60 min), copy the row into `tracking.tsv`. Read the domain profile: if dictation speed ceiling is low, arm the perception branch (§3); if the profile says fluent-but-thin, raise harvest to 5/day.
- Sunday (5 min): put the 5-week diagnostic re-run in the calendar now.

**Week 2 — full template live.**
- Mon: first harvest + first before-bed learning session.
- Tue: first AI voice session on the week's topic.
- Wed: first read-aloud/shadow sandwich.
- Thu: first tutor session — deliver the briefing script verbatim in the first two minutes.
- Fri: first week-close; confirm the playlist still has ≥5 downloaded episodes queued. If lunch comprehension is below roughly 80–90%, switch that topic's sources to learner-oriented content or add transcripts — comprehensible input requires comprehension.

## 8. Top 3 adherence risks for THIS plan, with mitigations

1. **Harvest decay.** The SRS-from-input link is the highest-friction element: pausing to capture words breaks listening flow, so it silently dies and the deck starves. *Mitigation:* capture is capped at 3–5 items, done from memory (or starred timestamps) in the evening slot only, never mid-listening; and the deck is allowed to run on backlog indefinitely — an empty harvest day costs nothing. If two straight weeks produce zero harvests, downgrade to one batch-harvest in the Friday week-close.
2. **Tutor-cancellation spiral.** The Thursday booking is the plan's only appointment; each cancellation makes the next easier, and drifting to AI-only interaction is comfortable — exactly the anxiety-avoidance loop the affect finding warns compounds against you. *Mitigation:* standing recurring slot with the same person (relationship = social cost of cancelling), prepaid in packages; and an automatic conversion rule: a cancelled tutor session becomes a 20-min AI voice session that same evening, so the two-conversation floor never breaks — but AI may substitute for the human at most one week in a row.
3. **Lunch-anchor volatility.** Work lunches and meetings will erase the 25-min engine slot several times a month, and the engine is 47% of the plan. *Mitigation:* episodes are pre-downloaded (no connectivity decision), and the slot has a declared fallback chain — lunch → commute → the evening slot's front end; the minimum-viable day's "press play for 7 attended minutes" defines the floor. The Friday queue check guarantees there is always something to press play on, which is the whole point of the design.
