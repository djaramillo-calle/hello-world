# DAILY ENGLISH ROUTINE — INTERACTION-FIRST

Designed for: adult intermediate learner, conversation goal, 45–60 min/weekday, anchors = morning / lunch / evening. All principle citations refer to the evidence review unless marked METHOD.

## 1. Philosophy in 3 sentences

Real conversational turns — where you must be understood, get stuck, and repair yourself — are the scarcest ingredient in an adult's week and the one that most directly builds the target skill, so this plan schedules interaction first and treats everything else (listening, vocabulary, drills) as fuel and warm-up for those turns. **Strongest evidence for:** interaction beats reception at equal time-on-task for speaking outcomes (2025 neuro-ecological study: interactive sessions outperformed matched non-interactive listening on both speaking *and* comprehension); the immersion-classroom production gap (near-native comprehension, weak production) shows a pure-input regime under-builds exactly what this learner wants; and self-initiated repair ("learning in the wild") is identified as unusually potent per second spent. **Strongest evidence against, honestly:** implicit exposure wins on delayed tests (g = 1.76 vs 0.77 explicit) and comprehensible-input volume is the best-evidenced driver of vocabulary and comprehension — by capping input at roughly a third of weekly minutes, this plan deliberately trades slower vocabulary breadth and listening range for faster speaking automatization, and accepts that the vocab and C-test metrics will move more slowly than they would under an input-first design.

Interaction-first justification: you cannot repair what you cannot hear.

## 3. Interaction architecture

**Count: 5 interactive engagements/week** — 2 AI voice sessions (Mon, Wed), 1 tutor session (Tue), 1 human call (Thu), plus the daily work micro-mission counted as one aggregate channel. That is ~95 minutes of real turn-taking weekly, roughly 35% of total time, with another ~20% in production-adjacent rehearsal (4/3/2, spoken SRS, shadowing).

**Briefing per channel — the prompts-not-recasts instruction, adapted** [oral corrective feedback: prompts > recasts, recasts d ≈ 0.35 and often unnoticed]:

- **AI (standing saved prompt, pasted every session):** "Voice conversation only. When I make an error, do NOT correct me or reformulate. Say 'sorry — what do you mean?' or ask me to say it again, and make me repair it myself. Every ten turns, stop and list the three errors I repeated most. Stay on [topic]. Push back on my opinions so I have to defend them." This converts the default recast machine into a prompt machine (verbatim mechanism from the review's LLM-partner section).
- **Tutor (in the booking message and re-sent monthly):** "Please don't reformulate my sentences. When something breaks, signal it — 'again?', a clarification request — and let me self-correct. Save the corrections you *must* give for a written list in the last 5 minutes. Each session: 10 minutes free conversation, then I retell one prepared story twice — interrupt the second telling only with prompts." (Task repetition inside the session upgrades accuracy and syntactic complexity on the second telling.)
- **Colleagues (unbriefable — convert yourself):** one scripted micro-mission per workday, written down in the morning slot: ask one real clarification question, volunteer the meeting summary, disagree once with a softener. The mission forces a transactional exchange with genuine stakes; getting stuck mid-turn and repairing is the point [in-the-wild; self-initiated repair]. Optionally recruit one trusted colleague: "if I say something unclear, ask me to rephrase instead of guessing."
- **Friend (light-touch, said once):** "Don't finish my sentences, and when I'm unclear just ask me to say it again — don't fix it for me. If you notice a mistake I keep making, tell me at the end, not during."

**Work-English conversion, passive → deliberate:** meetings you merely attend are background exposure (near-zero yield per the review's kill-list). The morning script + lunch debrief loop converts one moment per day into planned production with a repair opportunity, then harvests it: any phrase you reached for and lacked becomes a spoken SRS card that evening [retrieval; chunks].

**Chunk pipeline:** maintain 40–60 conversation-management chunks ("what I mean is…", "let me put it another way", "it depends", "I was going to say") in the SRS from week 1 — formulaic sequences are the main engine of fluent-sounding speech [chunks/formulaic sequences].

## 4. Minimum-viable day (≤15 minutes)

Rule: **a terrible day still contains one real speaking act.**
- 5′ spoken SRS (whichever anchor survives) + **10′ AI voice conversation** with the standing brief — no scheduling, no coordination, no judgment, which is exactly the anxiety-tolerant on-ramp the evidence supports [WTC/anxiety; AI as on-ramp].
- If speaking aloud is truly impossible (sick, shared room): 5′ silent SRS + 10′ narrow listening, and the day still counts — but two consecutive no-speaking days trigger the next day's AI session as non-negotiable.
Everything else — tutor, 4/3/2, shadowing, work missions — is droppable without breaking the chain. Missed tutor sessions are rebooked, never "made up" by doubling [spacing beats massing; streaks measure habit, not progress — the habit metric here is speaking acts, not app opens].

## 5. Measurement hooks (feeds METHOD.md's 4–6-week cycle)

- **Baseline:** diagnostic.html once in week 1 (~60 min), scores logged to tracking.tsv (battery v2 — v1 scores are non-comparable per form history).
- **Re-run:** every 4–6 weeks, weekend slot, same conditions; log to tracking.tsv each time. Quarterly: EF SET as the external anchor. Monthly: the authentic-audio check (weekend, 15′) — this plan's shadowing/listening work should shrink the "real-speech penalty" gap between it and the synthetic dictation score.
- **Which elements should move which metrics:**
  - 2× AI + tutor + 4/3/2 → **speech rate (wpm), filler ratio, self-repair rate** in the speaking sample — the plan's primary success metrics [4/3/2 raises rate and cuts hesitation; interaction volume].
  - Chunk SRS + task repetition → **elicited imitation** (reconstruction from proceduralized grammar; treat repeat scores as a floor per METHOD).
  - Spoken SRS + narrow listening → **yes/no vocabulary** and **beyond-core ratio / MTLD** — expect slow movement; the interaction-first tradeoff predicts these lag [honest cost of the philosophy].
  - Shadow sandwich / dictation drills → **partial dictation speed ceiling** and the monthly authentic-audio score.
  - Work missions + weekly human calls → **evidence inventory** (behavioral 14-day items) — this should saturate fastest and confirms the architecture is actually running.
- **Interpretation discipline:** only movement at or above METHOD's noise thresholds counts (C-test ±8, vocab ±1,100 families, dictation ±10, wpm ±12, MTLD ±8); repeat scores are floors due to item memory. Mid-cycle course corrections use the Friday log line only: conversations completed (target 5), work missions done (target 5), "was I pushed to self-repair? y/n" per session.

## 6. Onboarding, weeks 1–2

**Week 1 — baseline and channels:**
- **Day 1 (evening):** run diagnostic.html (~60 min — it replaces the whole day's budget). Log tracking.tsv. Note two outputs specifically: dictation speed ceiling (decides the perception add-on) and the named failure profile (e.g. knowledge–access gap vs fluent-but-thin — it tells you whether tutor sessions should push accuracy or volume) [METHOD interpretation rules].
- **Day 2:** set up AI voice; save the standing brief as a reusable prompt; do the first 10′ conversation the same day [start speaking in week one — the delay doctrine is the weakest-supported claim].
- **Day 3:** choose 4 narrow topics you will *genuinely* talk about — your work domain, one personal interest, one opinion/debate topic, daily-life/social — and pick one podcast/speaker per topic [narrow listening maps onto the real conversational goal]. Book the week-2 tutor slot with the brief pasted into the booking message.
- **Day 4:** build the initial SRS deck: 40–60 conversation-management chunks + everything harvested from Day 2's AI session; all cards production-format, reviewed aloud [chunks; production-format retrieval].
- **Day 5:** first scripted work micro-mission + second AI conversation. Weekend (optional): EF SET anchor, or defer it two weeks if the week felt heavy [affect is a mechanism — don't front-load overwhelm].

**Week 2 — full template live:**
- Run the Mon–Fri table as written. Tue: first tutor session — open by confirming the brief verbally, run the retell task. Thu: first human call (friend from personal circle if no exchange partner is booked yet; AI fallback fires automatically otherwise). Fri: first recorded 4/3/2 — archive the recording; it becomes your qualitative longitudinal artifact alongside tracking.tsv. Activate the perception add-on now if Day 1's dictation ceiling warranted it. End of week 2: first weekly log line, and lock the tutor slot as a recurring booking.

## 7. Top 3 adherence risks for THIS plan, with mitigations

1. **The interaction slots are the highest-friction slots, and they all sit in the tired evening anchor.** A conversation can be dodged in a way a podcast can't; this plan dies quietly if evenings erode. *Mitigation:* the tutor is a recurring paid booking (commitment device with a cancellation cost); AI sessions require zero coordination and are the designated degraded mode — the MVD explicitly counts a 10′ AI conversation as full success, so a bad week degrades to 3–5 short conversations instead of zero [WTC; AI on-ramp].
2. **Recast drift.** Tutors, AIs, and friends all revert to smooth reformulation because prompting feels rude, and the learner stops enforcing the brief because being made to repair is uncomfortable — silently swapping the plan's strongest feedback type for its weakest (d ≈ 0.35, often unnoticed). *Mitigation:* the brief is re-pasted at the start of *every* AI session (it's saved text — zero cost); the Friday log's "pushed to self-repair? y/n" per session makes drift visible; two consecutive "n" sessions on any channel trigger a re-brief, and a tutor who can't stop recasting after two re-briefs gets replaced.
3. **Silent skipping of work missions.** Nobody notices if you don't volunteer the summary, and speaking anxiety makes avoidance the default — removing the plan's only free daily human interaction [affect gates the practice you actually get]. *Mitigation:* the mission is scripted in the morning slot as one written sentence (tiny, concrete, pre-committed) and logged y/n at Thursday's debrief; three misses in a week means the mission difficulty is *downgraded* (from "disagree in the meeting" to "ask one clarification question"), never abandoned — the dial is difficulty, not existence.
