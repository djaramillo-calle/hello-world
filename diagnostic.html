English Signal Check

Six independent measures of what you can actually do in English, chosen because each one has published validity evidence and can be scored without a human examiner. Run the whole battery in about 60 minutes, or any single module in under 10.

What this measures, and what it can't

Read once~4 min

No free test measures conversational English accurately. The accurate ones are proctored and cost money; the free ones are mostly reading-and-listening multiple choice, which is the part of English you are least likely to be weak in. This battery works around that by measuring six narrower things that are each individually well-evidenced, and refusing to average them into a single number.

Read this before trusting any number here. The scores are calibrated against typical published values, not against a normed sample of people like you. Treat every band as provisional — ± one CEFR sub-level. What this battery is reliable for is change: because the items and the scoring are identical every time, a 10-point move on the C-test or a 15-wpm move in speech rate is real signal even if the absolute level is off.

How to keep it honest

  • Never look anything up mid-test, and don't retake a module the same day to improve a score.
  • Same conditions every time — same time of day if you can, no alcohol, not exhausted. Speech rate in particular moves with fatigue.
  • Re-run every 4–6 weeks. More often than that and you're measuring noise plus item memory; less often and you can't tell which change caused what.
  • Item memory is the real threat. By round three you will half-remember C-test answers. The battery flags this — treat repeat runs as a floor, not a level.

The external anchor

Once a quarter, take the EF SET (free, ~50 min, reading and listening, CEFR-calibrated with published correlation studies against TOEFL iBT). It is the only free test that is both standardised and CEFR-aligned. It won't tell you anything about your speaking, but it anchors the two receptive domains here to an external scale, which is exactly what a home-built instrument can't do for itself.

Module 1 — C-test

General proficiency~15 min0 / 80

Four passages of increasing difficulty. In each, the second half of every second word has been removed. Restore it. You are reconstructing from grammar, collocation and context at once — which is why this correlates with nearly every other measure of language ability.

Rules: exact spelling counts. Skip anything you can't get rather than stalling. Roughly 15 minutes for all four — don't let one gap eat two of them.

Module 2 — Vocabulary size

Breadth~6 min0 / 96

Mark every word you know the meaning of. Some of these words do not exist. They are there to measure how much you over-claim, and your score is corrected downward by exactly that amount — so guessing generously actively lowers your result.

"Know" means you could explain it or use it correctly in a sentence — not that it looks familiar. Work fast; first instinct is fine.

Module 3 — Dictation

Listening decode~8 min0 / 10

Ten sentences, played at increasing speed. Type exactly what you hear. This tests the bottleneck that actually breaks conversations: whether you can segment a fast stream into words in real time, not whether you understood the gist.

Synthetic voice caveat. This uses your browser's speech synthesis, which is clearer than real speech — no reductions, no overlap, no accent variety. Score high here and still struggle with a real speaker and that gap is the finding. Use the authentic-audio protocol in the report for the real check.

Module 4 — Elicited imitation

Oral encoding~8 min0 / 8

Listen to a sentence, decide whether it makes sense, then repeat it aloud from memory. Sentences longer than your working memory can hold verbatim can only be reproduced by reconstructing them from your grammar — which is why this predicts oral proficiency rather than memory span.

Say it out loud. Subvocalising inflates the score badly. The sense-judgement between hearing and repeating is deliberate — it stops you parroting the sound before it has been through your grammar.

Module 5 — Speaking sample

Output~15 minnot run

The one that matters most and the one no free test measures. You will record two minutes of unprepared speech, transcribe it verbatim, and get objective measures back: speech rate, filler load, self-repair, lexical diversity and lexical reach.

Module 6 — Evidence inventory

Real-world use~4 min0 / 14

Not "can you" — did you. Self-rated confidence correlates poorly with actual test performance, so every item here asks about a thing that either happened in the last two weeks or didn't. This is the module that tells you whether your English is growing or just being maintained.
